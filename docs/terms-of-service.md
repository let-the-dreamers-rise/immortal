## 2. Breath practices specifically

Some practices in the app involve slowing or holding the breath.

- **Never practise breath retention in or near water.** Breath-holding before or during swimming can cause blackout without warning and has killed experienced swimmers.
- **Never practise breath retention while driving** or operating anything where losing consciousness would be dangerous.
- **Do not hyperventilate before holding your breath.** Rapid deep breathing beforehand delays the urge to breathe without increasing your oxygen, so you can lose consciousness before your body warns you.
- Practise seated or lying down, somewhere you would be safe if you fainted.
- Breath retention is not appropriate during pregnancy, or with uncontrolled blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, epilepsy, or a history of panic attacks.

## 3. Meditation specifically

Intensive or prolonged meditation can produce difficult psychological experiences, including anxiety, depersonalisation, disturbed perception, and re-emerging traumatic memories. This is documented in the research literature and it is not rare.

If you have a personal or family history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, dissociative disorder, or severe trauma, speak to a mental health professional before undertaking intensive practice. If practice starts producing distressing experiences, stop and seek qualified help.
